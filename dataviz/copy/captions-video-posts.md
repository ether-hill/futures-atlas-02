## 1. Q-Day (`q-day-portrait.mp4` / `q-day-landscape.mp4`)

### Instagram

Will a quantum computer break RSA encryption within 10 years?

RSA protects a lot of what gets sent online, and a big enough quantum computer could crack it. Every year the Global Risk Institute and evolutionQ ask quantum experts how likely that is. In 2025, 26 of them answered. Each dot is one expert.

On the 10-year question they split down the middle: 13 said unlikely, 13 said about even or likely. Give it 30 years and 22 of the 26 say likely.

This is informed opinion from people in the field, not a measurement, and the panel changes from year to year.

## 2. Qubits vs quality (`qubits-vs-quality-portrait.mp4` / `-landscape.mp4`)

### Instagram

Post title: Which quantum computers make the fewest errors?

Which quantum computers make the fewest errors?

Going by the published numbers, Quantinuum's trapped-ion machines. Its 98-qubit Helios reports 0.79 errors per 1,000 two-qubit gates. Google's Willow, the best superconducting chip in our set, reports 3.3.

That error rate says more about a quantum computer than its qubit count, and some of the biggest machines don't publish it. IBM's 1,121-qubit Condor was announced with no average error at all.

A caveat on the source data: every number here was measured and reported by the company or lab that built the machine. Most are in peer-reviewed papers, but peer review checks the method, not whether the machine performs like that every day. Each maker tests in its own way, and these are best results. 14 machines from 2019 to 2026, hand-picked, not a census.

Should quantum companies have to report errors the same way, checked by someone else? Tell us below.

Full chart and sources at futures-atlas.com/futures-in-figures (link in bio)

Sources: 8 peer-reviewed papers (Nature, Physical Review Letters, Physical Review X) and 5 company documents from Google, IBM, Atom Computing and Microsoft

## 3. Training compute (`training-compute-portrait.mp4` / `-landscape.mp4`)

### Instagram

The biggest AI training runs grew about 4x a year.

That's Epoch AI's estimate for the running top 10 models between 2018 and 2024 (4.2x a year, 90% range 3.6x to 4.9x). Each dot is a model. The blue ones were the largest of their day.

The newest giants are the hardest to pin down. Labs often don't publish these numbers, so Epoch rebuilds them from the hardware and training time. From 2019 to 2022, 24 of 27 frontier estimates were rated confident. Since 2023 it's 5 of 27.

Compute isn't capability, cost or energy. It's how much calculation went into training.
